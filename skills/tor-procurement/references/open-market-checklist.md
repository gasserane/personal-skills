# Open-market ToR checklist

Proven on the AI-for-Research ToR v0.4→v0.7 cycle (July 2026). Four revision rounds kept re-hardening the same three fronts: (a) making the money publishable and donor-compliant, (b) making the open-market process defensible, (c) shifting from a closed drafting job to a validated, co-created method. This checklist starts the next ToR at v0.7 quality instead of reaching it through revisions.

In **build** mode: apply every item or record why it does not apply. In **review** mode: score against every item; each miss is a weak point. Clause language below is the proven v0.7 wording — adapt figures and dates, keep the mechanism.

## Canonical document structure

Front matter before section 1, annexes after section 13:

- Internal preparation note — REMOVE BEFORE PUBLICATION (strip list of unresolved items)
- At a glance (key-value table: title, commissioned by, leads, budget, level of effort, duration, location)
- How to apply (deadline, submit-to, what to submit, Q&A dates, interview dates)
- The assignment in brief
1. Background
2. Objectives
3. Scope of work (in AND out; fence sensitive boundaries explicitly)
4. Deliverables (table with acceptance criteria per deliverable; review SLA stated here)
5. Timeline and key milestones
6. Approach and methodology
7. Management, reporting and supervision
8. Budget and payment
9. Data protection, confidentiality and ownership of outputs
10. Consultant profile and eligibility
11. Submission of proposal
12. Evaluation criteria and selection method
13. Safeguarding and ethics
- Annexes (worked scope, key reference documents, decision-support material)

## A. Money — publishable and donor-compliant

1. **Budget-scope / day-rate realism.** Do not fix the level of effort. "Level of effort — proposed by the consultant: the financial proposal states the offered number of consultant days per deliverable. The evaluation assesses the realism of the offered days against the scope." Underbidding is the main evaluation watch point — name day-realism in the scoring criteria (technical approach AND value for money). Before publishing, sanity-check ceiling vs realistic senior days; flag the tension in the internal prep note if it exists.
2. **Bulk-vs-split budget presentation.** Publish one figure: "The maximum available budget for this assignment is EUR [X], inclusive of all fees, costs and any applicable VAT." Funding sources are not named in the published text; the internal split (per source, conversion rates, budget lines) lives in the implementation plan or prep note only.
3. **Donor-funding visibility as grant condition.** Where a funding source requires acknowledgement (EU co-funding statement and disclaimer), attach it to the specific deliverable it funds, not to the budget text: it is a condition of the grant, not a budget disclosure.
4. **Instalment schedule tied to spend deadlines.** Weight and time instalments so donor fiscal deadlines are met by construction (v0.7: 20% inception / 30% final mapping invoiced in 2026 for the EU spend deadline / 50% final products). State what each instalment is conditional on (acceptance, not submission).
5. **Payment terms and contracting placeholders.** "Within [30] days of receipt of a correct invoice"; contracting entity and invoicing confirmed at contracting; consultant keeps a per-deliverable time record. Square brackets on anything finance has not yet confirmed, mirrored in the prep note.

## B. Process defensibility — open-market mechanics

6. **Bidding window with Q&A round.** Minimum four weeks publication-to-deadline. "Written questions to [address] by [date]. Consolidated answers go to everyone who has asked a question or registered interest, by [date]." Q&A appears both in How to apply and as a timeline milestone. Name who covers Q&A if the lead is on leave.
7. **Eligibility and named personnel.** State who may apply (individuals, teams, firms). Key personnel are named in the proposal; "substitution or subcontracting beyond the named personnel needs IPPF's prior written agreement."
8. **Conflict of interest.** "Applicants declare any actual or potential conflict of interest with IPPF, its Member Associations or this assignment in their proposal."
9. **Technical threshold before financial.** Weighted criteria table (v0.7: technical approach 35, expertise 25, IPPF context 15, work samples/references 15, value for money 10). "Proposals scoring at least 70 percent of the available technical points proceed; financial proposals of the others stay unopened."
10. **Scored interviews with a practical scenario.** Top 2-3 interviewed; interview includes "a short practical scenario" drawn from the real assignment; the panel may adjust technical and expertise scores after interview; award to highest combined score. Interview dates published in the ToR.
11. **Page caps.** Technical proposal "maximum [10] pages, excluding CVs and work samples"; CVs "maximum two pages each." Caps protect the panel and level the field.
12. **Process-protection clauses.** All four: "IPPF may seek clarifications, may decide not to award, and may re-advertise. Submitting a proposal creates no entitlement to a contract." "Proposal preparation costs are not reimbursed." "All applicants are notified of the outcome once the selection concludes."

## C. Delivery protection — after signature

13. **Review SLA.** "IPPF returns one consolidated set of comments on each deliverable within five working days of submission, so review never becomes the bottleneck." Consolidated single reviewer voice through the technical lead — no parallel comment streams reaching the consultant.
14. **Change control.** "Scope refinements agreed at inception are absorbed within the contracted budget and the offered days. Any change beyond that needs a written contract variation agreed before the work starts."
15. **Acceptance criteria per deliverable.** The deliverables table carries acceptance criteria, not just titles and dates. Payment conditions reference acceptance.
16. **Scope fencing.** Scope of work states what is out as explicitly as what is in; fence sensitive boundaries (v0.7: AI on service-user or high-risk data out of scope) so no bidder can price the wrong assignment.

## D. Participation and ownership

17. **Participatory validation moments.** A reference group of the people the products are for "acts as an advisory sounding board and co-creator" — ownership, not just data extraction. Draft products go through validation workshops with a written comment window after, and feedback is incorporated "with a brief comment-response log showing what changed and what did not, and why." Name participatory capability in the evaluation criteria if the method demands it.
18. **IP assignment despite co-creation.** "All deliverables and underlying materials are the property of IPPF. The consultant assigns all intellectual property in the outputs to IPPF" — reaffirmed explicitly against any co-creation arrangement.

## E. Compliance and hygiene

19. **Data protection and responsible AI.** Align to current IPPF IT policy, data management strategy, privacy notice, GDPR or comparable. Recordings and data on IPPF systems, not personal drives; local copies deleted at contract end. Responsible-AI disclosure expectations stated where relevant.
20. **Safeguarding and ethics section.** IPPF Safeguarding Policy named; research-ethics route named where the assignment touches research with people.
21. **Internal preparation note.** A REMOVE-BEFORE-PUBLICATION note at the top lists every unresolved item (calendar anchors, budget/VAT confirmation, contracting entity, discretionary choices like funding visibility). Unconfirmed facts go here in square brackets — never silently into the published text (factual-reliability rule).
22. **Findability front matter.** At-a-glance table and How-to-apply box before the narrative, so a bidder can decide to bid in sixty seconds.

## Adaptation notes

- **Service contracts** (agency delivers a service, not consultant days): items 1 and 10 shift — realism scoring moves to unit rates or milestones, and the practical scenario tests the service, not the person. Everything else holds.
- **Single-source / waiver procurement**: competition items (6, 9, 10, 11, 12) fall away; the waiver justification replaces them. Money, delivery-protection, participation, and compliance items all still apply.
