# Quality bar — the acceptance floor for every output

Outputs from this skill must be **at least as good as** the reference deliverables produced for
the SECS Romania ARV review (the desk-review document with reviewer conclusions, the
per-principle standalone docs, and the pre-interview + interview lists). Treat the criteria and
worked exemplars below as the floor, not the ceiling. If a draft does not clear every applicable
checklist item, revise before delivering.

## Acceptance checklist

### Check-level reviewer conclusion (Evidence cell)
- [ ] Leads with the **supporting self-assessment evidence** ("The MA reports / cites …"), then
      the reviewer judgement ("Reviewer: …"). The reader sees what the MA claims *and* what the
      reviewer concludes.
- [ ] Cites **specific evidence**: article numbers, policy names, file names, dates — not
      "the documents".
- [ ] Carries a **status label** from the controlled vocabulary (see `ippf-arv-standards.md`).
- [ ] **Surfaces any discrepancy** between the MA's claim/verdict and what the evidence shows,
      rather than smoothing it over.
- [ ] **Flags data gaps** (scanned, unreadable, or missing documents) explicitly.
- [ ] Adds **specific, answerable interview questions** only where something genuinely needs
      probing — not boilerplate on every check.

### Standard-level conclusion (DESCRIPTION cell — the IPPF reader reads only this)
- [ ] **Self-contained.** Synthesises every check beneath the standard; the reader needs nothing
      else. Opens with `Desk-review status: …`.
- [ ] Structured as: what is evidenced (key evidence pulled up) → the gaps/qualifications →
      a closing judgement.
- [ ] **Cross-references** related standards where the same issue recurs (e.g. a missing results
      framework spans the strategic-plan and the M&E standards).
- [ ] Surfaces **cross-cutting findings** (e.g. the constitution describing service delivery the
      MA says it no longer runs, which reopens an "applicability" question).
- [ ] Ends with **consolidated interview questions** drawn from the checks.

### Pre-interview + interview lists
- [ ] **Two sections**: (A) pre-interview clarifications / document requests from the ED;
      (B) interview-phase questions.
- [ ] Every item tagged with the **check reference** and a **type**: `NC` (non-compliance / gap)
      or `CL` (clarification — standard met, evidence/confirmation needed).
- [ ] Grouped by principle (banner rows); interview items carry a **"To whom"** routing
      (Board / ED / Finance / Comms / Admin).
- [ ] Document requests are driven by the **data gaps** and the missing-evidence flags from the
      review, not invented.
- [ ] Any item sourced from outside the authoritative doc is **labelled** as such.

### Voice and format (both tiers)
- [ ] Tier 1 working brief: active voice, sentences under 25 words, plain English, Anglo-Saxon
      over Latinate, acronyms spelled out on first use.
- [ ] **No em-dashes in body prose**; hyphens in status labels.
- [ ] Reviewer text in purple `RGB(0x70,0x30,0xA0)`; status labels and headings bold.
- [ ] **Smallest font 10 pt** in generated companion documents.
- [ ] Nothing fabricated — no invented article, date, name, verdict or document content.

## Worked exemplars (this is the bar)

**Standard-level — a non-compliance done well (Standard 5.2):**
> REVIEWER CONCLUSIONS: Non-compliance with justification
> Risk management is partly in place but not compliant, and the MA accepts this. SECS has a
> substantive Risk Management Strategy — covering safety and security, duty of care, an annual
> risk register, and a business-continuity model with PESTLE-type factors including technology
> and finance — and, following an IPPF-supported resilience workshop, developed a Safety and
> Security Plan with an external expert … On this basis checks 5.2.1-5.2.3 are well evidenced …
> The genuine gaps are in IT and data security (5.2.4) and business continuity (5.2.5). The MA
> candidly reports no written password or account policy … A credible remediation roadmap
> already exists in the Safety and Security Plan, which itself prescribes the fixes.
> Questions / aspects for the interview:
> - Agree a prioritised, time-bound plan to implement the … digital-security and
>   business-continuity recommendations …

Why it clears the bar: self-contained, synthesises all five checks, separates what is evidenced
from the genuine gaps, names the remediation already on the table, ends with an actionable
question.

**Check-level — surfacing an MA-vs-evidence discrepancy (5.1.2):**
> REVIEWER CONCLUSIONS: Met
> The MA states the Strategic Plan includes the lines of action, expected results, an estimated
> timetable and the persons responsible. Reviewer: on review, the submitted 2025-2028 Strategy
> and Action Plan contains the lines of action but not the expected results, estimated timeline
> or named responsible persons.
> Questions / aspects for the interview:
> - Can you submit a more detailed work plan to complement the strategy, clearly indicating
>   expected results, estimated timeline and responsible persons?

Why it clears the bar: shows the MA claim and the contradicting evidence finding side by side,
then turns the gap into a concrete document request.

**Check-level — a missing-document flag (4.1.4):**
> REVIEWER CONCLUSIONS: Met
> The MA cites the Donor Accountability policy, point III … Reviewer: SECS declares that the
> Donor Accountability policy (reviewed) is explicit on the supremacy of SECS and IPPF policies,
> and the dual Board-and-ED sign-off is a real control. Nevertheless, this policy does not appear
> in the list of evidence provided so far.
> Questions / aspects for the interview:
> - Ask for the SECS Donor Accountability policy as evidence.

**Interview list — a tagged, routed row:**
> | 9.1 / 9.2 | NC | KEY: Do the statutory service offices operate, and does SECS hold any
> standing service point? Agree whether Standard 9.1 is applicable (referral-based access)
> rather than Not Applicable … | Board / ED |

## Self-check before delivery

Before returning any deliverable, re-read it once against this checklist. Two failure patterns
to watch for: (1) check conclusions that assert a judgement without the supporting MA evidence,
and (2) standard-level conclusions that summarise instead of synthesising — a reader who sees
only the DESCRIPTION cell must still get the full picture. If either appears, revise.
