# Stage 2 — INTERVIEW: extract the note by dictation

Ane talks, you structure. She will usually dictate by voice, so apply the meeting-notes transcription rules: read speech-to-text output charitably ("agent's" for "agents", stray punctuation, homophones), confirm every name and number rather than "correcting" from a pattern, and flag anything unverifiable with `⚠️` instead of smoothing it over.

## Method (grill-mel pattern)

- **One question per message.** Multiple questions bewilder a dictating speaker.
- **Recommend first, then ask.** State your recommended answer with its one load-bearing reason, so Ane reacts to a position, not a blank page.
- **Read instead of asking.** If the answer already exists (ledger, content folder, memory, conversation), go read it and confirm what you found.
- **Stop when the skeleton stands** or when Ane says enough. This interview is shorter than a grill-mel: typically 6-10 questions.

## The branches to resolve

Walk these in order; skip any the dictation already answered.

1. **The story.** What concrete moment opens the piece? Two sentences maximum; the finding must land by sentence three. If Ane has no story, a blunt claim opening works (field note #01 opened with the claim, not a story).
2. **The one-line thesis.** What is the single claim the note argues? This becomes the title and the hook line of the post.
3. **The lessons.** 3-5 lessons, each with its load-bearing reason and one concrete example from her practice. Get the order: strongest first or building to strongest?
4. **The candour item.** What did not work, or what can the evidence not show? Every field note carries one; it is the series' honesty signal.
5. **The numbers.** Which figures will the piece cite (agent counts, checks, pages, months)? Verify each against source now, or mark `⚠️ verify before posting` in the draft header.
6. **The toolkit promise.** What one attachable artifact does the article promise the reader? What must be in it for a reader to use it the same afternoon?
7. **The closing question.** What does Ane genuinely want to hear from readers? The CTA is a real question, not engagement bait.
8. **The self-irony moment.** At most one, usually in the P.S. Ask only if the dictation offered none.

## Output of the stage

A confirmed skeleton in conversation (not yet a file): thesis, story, ordered lessons with examples, candour item, verified numbers, toolkit promise, closing question, P.S. angle. Update the ledger entry (status stays `drafted pipeline`, add the skeleton decisions), then move to Stage 3.
