# Indicator set: youth contraception access project (3 peri-urban districts)

**Use six indicators: four output indicators the clinics report every six months, and two outcome indicators the household surveys measure at baseline and endline.** The outcome sentence has two parts, "use" and "the method they choose". Without a separate choice indicator, a rise in use would not show that young people got the method they wanted.

## Executive summary

1. Keep "number of young people receiving contraceptive services", but redefine it. Paper registers usually count visits, not people.
2. Outcome indicators appear only twice (baseline and endline). The four output indicators carry the six-monthly reporting and the mid-project adjustments.
3. Indicator 5 adapts SDG 3.7.1 (demand satisfied with modern methods) to ages 15 to 24. The standard version covers women aged 15 to 49.
4. Indicator 6 is bespoke. It measures "the method they choose" directly, and no standard indicator covers that wording.
5. Five of six indicators are bespoke (Tier 3). That is expected for project-delivery indicators, but the household survey must be designed carefully because it is your only outcome evidence.

## Indicator notes

I assumed the plan means one evening session and one Saturday session per clinic per week. Please confirm this, because Indicator 2 depends on it.

### 1. Young people aged 15-24 receiving a contraceptive service (output, your existing indicator, redefined)

- **Definition:** Count of different young people aged 15 to 24 who received at least one contraceptive service (method supplied, method prescribed, or counselling that ended in a method decision) in the six-month period.
- **Counted as:** A count, with no denominator.
- **Disaggregation:** Age (15-19, 20-24), sex, new or returning client, youth session or regular hours, clinic. Add disability status (Washington Group Short Set, 6 questions on difficulty seeing, hearing, walking, remembering, self-care and communicating) only if the clinic can ask it at registration. Otherwise record it as a gap.
- **Data source:** Monthly clinic register summaries.
- **Reliability: medium.** A young person who visits three times is counted three times, unless the register carries a client number. If it does not, report "visits by young people" and say so. Also, the clinics already serve young people, so the total will not show what the project added. The "youth session versus regular hours" split and the "new client" split show that.
- **Worked example:** Clinic A reports 400 visits by young people, of which 90 came during youth sessions and 60 were new clients. The 60 new clients tell you more about reach than the 400.
- **Quality check:** The clinic manager compares the monthly summary with the paper register for one randomly chosen week each quarter.
- **Tier 3:** local indicator kept at your request.

### 2. Youth sessions held as planned (output)

- **Definition:** Number of youth-friendly sessions held, divided by the number planned, in the period, per clinic.
- **Disaggregation:** Clinic, evening or Saturday.
- **Data source:** Simple session log (date, start time, staff on duty, young people seen).
- **Reliability: high.** Staff record this at the time of the session.
- **Use for adjustment:** Cancelled sessions show a staffing or opening-hours problem within weeks, not at endline.
- **Tier 3:** project delivery indicator.

### 3. Trained peer referrers who are active (output)

- **Definition:** Number of community health workers who completed the training and made at least one referral in the last three months, divided by 30 trained.
- **Disaggregation:** District, sex of referrer, age group of referrer.
- **Data source:** Training attendance sheets and referral slip returns.
- **Reliability: medium.** "Active" depends on slips being returned.
- **Use for adjustment:** If activity drops after month three, the programme team can add refresher visits or supervision.
- **Tier 3.**

### 4. Peer referrals that led to a clinic visit (output)

- **Definition:** Number of referral slips from peer referrers that match a clinic visit within 30 days, divided by the number of slips issued.
- **Disaggregation:** District, age group and sex of the young person referred, referrer.
- **Data source:** Numbered referral slips. Clinic staff tick a box in the register when a young person arrives with a slip.
- **Reliability: low to medium.** Young people may lose slips or arrive without them. Treat the result as a minimum.
- **Use for adjustment:** This is the best early signal that the peer model works. If many referrals never arrive, check travel, session times, or fear of being seen at the clinic.
- **Tier 3.**

### 5. Sexually active young people who want to avoid pregnancy and use a modern method (outcome)

- **Definition:** Proportion of young people aged 15 to 24 who had sex in the last 3 months and want to avoid a pregnancy (or avoid making someone pregnant), who currently use a modern contraceptive method (including condoms).
- **Numerator:** Those in the denominator who use a modern method now.
- **Denominator:** All young people aged 15 to 24 in the survey who had sex in the last 3 months and want to avoid pregnancy.
- **Disaggregation:** Sex, age (15-19, 20-24), marital status, district, disability status (Washington Group Short Set).
- **Interaction to look at:** Unmarried girls aged 15 to 19, and young people with a disability. These groups often face the biggest barriers (parental consent, provider attitudes, physical access).
- **Data source:** Household survey, baseline and endline only.
- **Reliability: medium.** Self-reported sexual activity among unmarried adolescents is often under-reported. The denominator is only part of the 12,000 young people, so a small sample will give wide margins of error. Ask a statistician for a sample size calculation before you budget the survey.
- **Tier 2:** adapted from SDG 3.7.1, which covers women aged 15 to 49. The adaptation extends it to young men, shifts the age band, and uses a shorter sexual-activity window.
- **Cost flag:** The survey is the main expense. Use the same questions and sampling method at baseline and endline, or the two rounds cannot be compared.

### 6. Young people using a modern method who say it is the method they chose (outcome)

- **Definition:** Proportion of young people aged 15 to 24 currently using a modern method who say they chose that method themselves (not chosen by a partner, parent or provider) and that it was the method they wanted.
- **Numerator:** Current users who answer yes to both questions.
- **Denominator:** All current users of a modern method in the survey.
- **Disaggregation:** Same as Indicator 5.
- **Data source:** Same household survey.
- **Reliability: medium to low.** Two risks. Young people may give socially expected answers, and the number of users in the sample may be small. Pilot the two questions with a few young people first.
- **Tier 3:** wording built to match the donor's outcome statement. A standard indicator exists for part of this idea (the Method Information Index in quality-of-care surveys), but I could not verify its source in this session. Check it with your MEL focal point before you decide whether to adapt it.

## What this set cannot tell you

1. **No outcome data between baseline and endline.** Indicators 1 and 4 act as early warnings only. Rising numbers of new clients and falling referral losses suggest progress but do not show that use increased. ⚠️ Data gap: outcome change in months 7 to 18, with no third survey — a clinic-only signal cannot reach young people who never attend — use the "new client" share in Indicator 1 as a proxy and state this limit in each donor report.
2. **No proof the project caused any change.** With no comparison group, endline results show change over time, not cause. Describe results as "consistent with the project contributing", not as proof.
3. **Gender identity and sexual orientation are not tracked.** Registers rarely record them and surveys need careful wording. ⚠️ Data gap: LGBTQI+ young people — not captured by clinic or survey tools as described — decide with the MA whether to add an optional, safe question, or document the exclusion.
4. **Interaction effects may be too small to analyse.** Sample sizes at endline may not support breakdowns such as "unmarried girls aged 15-19 with a disability" in each district. Collect the variables anyway, so you can pool districts later.
5. **Safeguarding and consent.** Interviewing 15 to 17 year-olds about sex needs ethics review and a clear local rule on consent. Check this before you finalise the survey budget.

## Measurement mechanism

Clinic staff fill in registers, session logs and referral slips. The clinic manager checks one sample week per quarter. The programme team sums the data monthly for each six-month report. Community health worker supervisors collect the slip returns. An enumerator team (external or MA staff who do not work in these clinics) runs the baseline and endline household surveys.
