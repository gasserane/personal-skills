## Intersectionality note

The set tracks young women and young men and three age bands side by side, but the registers and surveys cannot show how factors combine. The groups most likely to be missed are unmarried girls aged 15-17, young people with disabilities, and young men, who mostly seek condoms and may not come to clinics at all. After the endline survey, look at one pairing only: age band by sex for Indicators 4 and 5. Cutting further (for example girls aged 15-17 with a disability in one district) will leave too few people to report. Say that plainly in the report rather than showing figures built on a handful of respondents.

## How each indicator is measured

1. **Sessions held:** the clinic manager keeps a one-line log (date, start and end time, staff present). The district coordinator checks it against the rota monthly. The team uses it to spot clinics where sessions are slipping.
2. **Young people receiving services:** clinic staff record age, sex, service and method in the register. The data officer counts each person once per period by client number and compares totals with the monthly summary. The team compares youth sessions with routine hours to see where young people actually come.
3. **Attended after referral:** peer referrers hand out slips with their code. Clinic staff keep the slips and tick the register. The programme officer matches the slip count to the register count each month. The team uses it to see which referrers bring people in.
4. **Modern method use:** a survey team runs the household survey at baseline and endline with a standard questionnaire. The survey supervisor re-visits a sample of households to check answers. Include the Washington Group Short Set. This is the only indicator that costs real money, so protect the budget for both rounds.
5. **Own-choice method:** the same survey, asking two plain questions to current users: "Did you pick this method yourself?" and "Did anyone tell you about other methods?" The same supervisor checks. The team uses the endline result to improve counselling.
6. **Return visits:** the data officer matches client numbers across register pages. The clinic manager reviews unmatched cases quarterly. A low return rate tells the team to look at follow-up reminders or side-effect counselling.

## Tier distribution

Tier 1 (a globally validated indicator used as is): 0. Tier 2 (a validated indicator adapted to this project): 2 (Indicators 2 and 4). Tier 3 (new for this project): 4 (Indicators 1, 3, 5 and 6). More than one in five indicators is new, so the set may be building measures that already exist. In this case the new ones count things only this project does (sessions, peer referrals, return visits) or measure choice, which standard global indicators cover only in part. Check the global lists before you finalise.

## What this set cannot tell you

1. **Cause.** There is no comparison group. If use goes up between baseline and endline, the set cannot show that the project caused it. Other things may change in the same two years.
2. **Timing.** The two outcome indicators report twice in two years. Donor reports at months 6, 12 and 18 will show activity and clinic numbers only.
3. **Total use.** Clinic registers miss young people who get contraception from pharmacies or other clinics. Indicator 4 comes from the surveys and covers them, but Indicator 2 does not.
4. **Groups not tracked.** Disability appears only in the surveys. Young people who never attend a clinic show up only in the survey, and only if their household is sampled.
5. **Small subgroups.** With about 12,000 young people in the area, a survey of a few hundred people can show a large change overall (roughly 10 percentage points, on my rough arithmetic with typical assumptions). It cannot show change by district or for the 15-17 group. A statistician should set the sample size before you cost the survey.
6. **Consent and safety.** Survey and register questions on sexual activity need care. Check the national rules on consent for 15-17 year olds, set up private interviews, and do not link names to answers. Referral slips must not reveal why a young person is attending.
7. **Quality of service.** Indicator 5 captures choice and information. It does not measure whether young people felt respected. A short client feedback question at the clinic would fill this gap, but it falls outside the data sources you listed.
