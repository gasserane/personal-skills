**Bottom line:** Use six indicators: three outputs from clinic registers and three outcomes from the two household surveys. Your existing indicator stays, but only after one fix: it must count each young person once. The donor outcome ("use the method they choose") needs two survey indicators, because "use" and "choose" are different things. Only the clinic registers can tell the team how delivery is going between surveys, so they carry the six-monthly adjustment.

## The six indicators

| # | Indicator | Level | Data source | When |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Number of youth-friendly sessions delivered, as a share of the sessions planned | Output | Clinic session log | Monthly, reported every 6 months |
| 2 | Number of young people aged 15-24 receiving contraceptive services (your existing indicator, tightened) | Output | Clinic registers | Monthly, reported every 6 months |
| 3 | Number of young people who reach a clinic after a peer referrer sends them | Output | Referral slips, matched to the register | Monthly, reported every 6 months |
| 4 | Proportion of sexually active young people who want to avoid pregnancy and use a modern contraceptive method | Outcome | Household survey | Baseline and endline |
| 5 | Proportion of young contraceptive users who chose their own method and got the method they wanted | Outcome | Household survey | Baseline and endline |
| 6 | Proportion of young people who know where to get a method and say they could get it if they wanted | Outcome (access) | Household survey | Baseline and endline |

### 1. Sessions delivered against plan
- **Definition:** Evening or Saturday sessions that actually ran, divided by the sessions planned (one per week per clinic).
- **Disaggregate by:** clinic and district.
- **Reliability: high.** Staff record it directly.
- **Why it matters:** If sessions are cancelled, every other number will fall. This is the first thing to check when results look weak.

### 2. Young people receiving contraceptive services
- **Definition:** Young people aged 15-24 who received a contraceptive method, or counselling that led to one, at the three clinics. Count each person once per six-month period.
- **Disaggregate by:**
  - age (15-19 and 20-24; split 15-17 from 18-19 if the register allows);
  - sex;
  - method;
  - session type (youth session or regular clinic hours);
  - clinic.
- **Reliability: medium.** Paper registers summarised monthly usually count visits, not people. A young person who returns for an injection three times is counted three times. Add a client ID (initials plus birth year) or report "visits" and "new clients" as two separate numbers.
- **Why the session split matters:** It shows whether the youth sessions reach new young clients or only move existing ones from regular hours.
- **Disability:** Registers cannot collect disability status ethically without staff training. ⚠️ Data gap: disability status is missing from the register. Measure it in the survey with the Washington Group questions (Indicators 4 to 6).

### 3. Young people reaching a clinic after peer referral
- **Definition:** Young people who arrive with a referral slip from one of the 30 trained peer referrers, counted in the period of arrival.
- **Disaggregate by:** district, age group, and referrer (each referrer gets a code on the slip).
- **Reliability: medium to low.** Slips get lost, and some young people will not show one. Treat the figure as a minimum.
- **Why it matters:** It shows which referrers work and which do not, so the team can coach or reassign them. Also report how many of the 30 referrers made at least one referral that period.
- **Safeguarding:** The slip must carry a code only. It must not carry a name or the reason for the visit.

### 4. Modern contraceptive use among young people who want to avoid pregnancy
- **Definition:** Sexually active young people aged 15-24 who do not want a pregnancy now and who use a modern method (themselves or their partner).
- **Numerator and denominator:** Users of a modern method, divided by all sexually active young people who want to avoid pregnancy. This includes those not using any method.
- **Tier 2.** It adapts SDG indicator 3.7.1, which covers women aged 15-49 and measures "demand satisfied by modern methods". This version covers ages 15-24 and men and women.
- **Disaggregate by:** age (15-17 and 18-24), sex, district, marital status, and disability (Washington Group Short Set).
- **Reliability: medium.** It is self-reported and sensitive. Under-reporting among unmarried girls is likely.
- **Limit:** The two surveys sample different people, so this shows a change in the area. It does not prove the project caused the change.

### 5. Chose their own method and got it
- **Definition:** Current young users who say they picked their method themselves and received the method they wanted.
- **Numerator and denominator:** Users who say both, divided by all current young users.
- **Tier 3.** I did not find a validated 15-24 version with an open source. Draft two plain survey questions ("Who decided on the method?" and "Did you get the method you wanted?") and pilot them with young people.
- **Disaggregate by:** age, sex, district, and whether the user attends youth sessions.
- **Reliability: medium to low.** This is the only indicator that captures "the method they choose", so keep it despite the weakness. Explain the weakness to the donor.
- **Small numbers:** Current users are a subset of respondents. District figures will be too small to read, so report this for the whole area only.

### 6. Know where to get a method, and could get one
- **Definition:** Young people aged 15-24 who name a place where they could get a method and say they could get one if they wanted.
- **Tier 3.** It measures access, which is your project's stated aim, and it does not depend on sexual activity. All young people answer it.
- **Disaggregate by:** age, sex, district, disability.
- **Reliability: medium.** The sample is larger than for Indicators 4 and 5, so this is the likeliest outcome to show a change at endline.

## What this set cannot do

1. **No mid-project outcome data.** Outcomes appear only at baseline and endline. Use Indicators 1 to 3 for six-monthly adjustment, and tell the donor this in the proposal.
2. **Tier mix.** Five of six indicators are Tier 3, which is above the 20% flag. No global standard exists for session or referral counts, and Indicator 4 is anchored to SDG 3.7.1. The risk is that the survey questions for 5 and 6 are untested.
3. **Sample size.** ⚠️ Data gap: with 12,000 young people, the survey sample must be calculated before you commit to district figures. Ask a statistician. Only young people who are sexually active answer Indicators 4 and 5, so those groups will be small. Plan for results by age group and for the whole area, and treat district results as indicative.
4. **Consent and ethics.** Asking 15-17 year-olds about sexual activity needs ethics review and a consent rule that matches national law. Settle this before the baseline.

## Intersectionality note

The set tracks three interactions:
- Age and sex: girls aged 15-17 are the group most likely to face consent and judgement barriers.
- Disability and age, in the survey only.
- District and session type: this shows whether evening and Saturday slots reach working and school-age youth.

The set does not track LGBTQI+ young people. Asking about sexual orientation in a household survey risks harm to respondents, so it is left out deliberately. Learn about that group through peer referrers' feedback and a short consultation instead. Interaction analysis in the survey will be too thin beyond two-way splits. Say so in the proposal rather than promise more.

## Who does what

- **Clinic staff** complete the register and session log. **The programme officer** compiles monthly figures and checks for double counting. **Peer referrers** hand out coded slips.
- **A contracted survey team** runs the baseline and endline, using the same questions both times. **A staff member** spot-checks 10% of register entries each quarter.
- **Cost:** The surveys are the main cost. The register changes (client ID, referral code, session-type tick box) cost almost nothing.

**Tier count:** Tier 1: 0. Tier 2: 1 (Indicator 4). Tier 3: 5 (Indicators 1, 2, 3, 5, 6).
