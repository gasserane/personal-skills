## Decided

1. **Workshop format.** A half-day workshop for youth volunteers aged 18 to 24 on how to collect feedback from young clients after a clinic visit.
2. **Agenda (version 2).** The role play stays, but it is cut to 45 minutes. The reason: some volunteers are nervous about talking to clients, so they need practice, but not too much. A 15-minute consent session comes before the survey practice.

   | 11:30 | Role play on hard conversations (45 min) |
   | 12:15 | Next steps |
   | 12:30 | Close |

3. **Exit survey: no more than 5 questions, in a paper version and a phone version.**
   1. How easy was it to get an appointment?
   2. Did staff treat you with respect?
   3. Did you get the information you needed?
   4. Did you feel safe during your visit?
   5. Anything else you want to tell us?
4. **Question 4 replaced.** The old question ("Would you recommend us to a friend?") sounded like a sales survey. The director asked last month for a question on whether young people felt safe.
5. **Answer scale 1 to 5 for questions 1 to 4.** The reason: it should match last year's survey so the results can be compared. This holds only once the check in open action 2 is done.
6. **The role play uses an invented case, not a real one.** Pattern: a young client leaves before seeing the doctor because the waiting room does not feel private. A real client story was suggested and then dropped, because using it would put that client's privacy at risk. This note leaves out that client's details on purpose. Do not add them back.

## To settle before continuing

1. **Can volunteers ask clients under 18 for feedback without a parent's consent? (new)** The clinic sees clients younger than 18. Your national law and your organisation's safeguarding policy decide this. The answer changes what the consent session teaches, and it may mean some clients cannot be asked at all.
2. **What does a volunteer do if a client answers "no" or gives a low score on question 4? (new)** A low answer on safety may point to a safeguarding concern. If the answer is "refer to a named staff member", the consent session or the next-steps session needs 5 minutes on that referral route.
3. **What if last year's survey used a 1-to-4 scale? (new)** You would have to choose: switch to 1 to 4 and keep the comparison, or keep 1 to 5 and lose it. Decide this before the translator starts.
4. **Where and how will volunteers ask the questions? (new)** The role-play case is about a waiting room that is not private. If volunteers ask the survey in that same waiting room, the survey repeats the problem. For the phone version, you also need to decide what personal data is collected and where answers are stored.
