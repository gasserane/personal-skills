## Working Theory of Change: Contraceptive choice for young women in two informal settlements

**Short answer:** The project works if three barriers fall together: no nearby service, unkind clinic staff, and permission from other people. The riskiest assumption is that peer educators, clinic training and family sessions will reach the young women who depend most on others. Nobody has yet checked who holds power over these decisions, so the first three months should do that.

## Vision

By the end of the project, and beyond it, young women aged 15 to 24 in both settlements decide whether and when to use contraception, and get it without shame or anyone else's permission. The project contributes to this change. It will not complete it alone in three years.

## Context

About 9,000 young women live in the two settlements. Many left school early, work informally, and rely on a partner or relative for money and permission to travel. The nearest public clinic is a 40-minute walk or a costly bus ride, and staff are often unkind to unmarried young women. The MA has 40 interviews and no earlier theory of change. The project cannot change national law or pay for transport.

## Main pathway (worked backwards from the vision)

**By end of year 3 (medium term)**
1. Young women know their options and feel able to ask for contraception.
2. Young women reach a service close to home, without a costly trip.
3. Staff at the two public clinics treat unmarried young women respectfully.
4. Partners and mothers-in-law stop blocking, or start supporting, a young woman's choice.

**By month 12 (short term)**
- 24 peer educators are trained and active in both settlements.
- The mobile clinic runs weekly in each settlement.
- Clinic staff have completed the respectful-care course.
- Family sessions are running monthly, and at least some partners and relatives attend.

**How the links work**
- Peer educators build trust and knowledge. The mobile clinic then removes the distance barrier that transport costs would otherwise create.
- Training changes how clinic staff treat young women, so those who use the public clinics are no longer turned away or shamed.
- Family sessions reduce open opposition, so a young woman can act on her choice.

## Assumptions we are making

The table shows the evidence behind each link. "Untested" means we have no evidence from this context yet.

| Link | Assumption (outside our control) | Evidence status |
|---|---|---|
| Peer educators → knowledge and confidence | Educators reach the most isolated young women, not only the well-connected | Plausible; untested here |
| Mobile clinic → access | Visiting the mobile clinic is private enough that a young woman is not exposed to her family | Untested |
| Clinic training → respectful care | Staff change behaviour, not only knowledge, and trained staff stay in post | Untested |
| Family sessions → less blocking | The people who attend are the ones who matter, and sessions do not trigger backlash or violence against young women | Untested; safety risk |
| All links → choice | Contraceptive supply is reliable, and no method is withheld at the point of care | Untested |

⚠️ Data gap: no evidence yet that any link holds in these two settlements. The 40 interviews describe barriers but do not test the pathway.

## Who holds power (first reading, to be checked)

⚠️ Feminist political economy analysis missing. The list below is a hypothesis drawn from the project design, not a finding.

- **Partners and relatives** control money, travel and permission. They may lose status if a young woman gains choice, so expect resistance.
- **Mothers-in-law** may be gatekeepers and also allies. Their interests differ from those of partners.
- **Clinic staff** control access at the point of care. Unkindness may reflect moral views, workload or fear of community judgment.
- **Employers** may control a young woman's time and mobility. The project does not currently address them.
- **Peer educators** are young women too. They may face the same pressures and risk being blamed.

The 40 interviews should be reread for who was interviewed. If only women already using services spoke, the most dependent young women are missing from the evidence.

## Threats to this theory

Other things could produce the same change without the project: other NGO or government programmes, new clinic hours, a social-media shift in attitudes, or an economic change that raises women's income. We must record these so we do not claim credit that belongs elsewhere.

## Contribution question

To what extent and in what ways did the project contribute to young women's contraceptive choice in the two settlements, given other contributions and context?

## Plan to test the riskiest assumptions

**Months 1 to 3: power mapping.** Hold small group discussions with young women, including some who did not take part in the 40 interviews. Ask who decides about money, travel and contraception. Interview 4 to 6 partners and mothers-in-law, and clinic staff, with safeguarding review first. Revise this theory of change with the results.

| Assumption | Confirming evidence | Disconfirming evidence | Source | Revisit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Educators reach isolated young women | Contacts include young women who need permission to travel | Contacts are mainly educators' friends and peers | Educator contact log; 10 follow-up conversations | Month 6 |
| Mobile clinic is private enough | Young women say they can attend without family finding out | Women avoid it for fear of being seen | Exit conversations; non-attender interviews | Month 6 |
| Staff behaviour changes | Young women report respectful care | Reports of shaming continue after training | Short exit survey at clinics; mystery-visit style check | Month 12 |
| Family sessions help, not harm | Attendees report fewer blocks; no safety incidents | Any rise in conflict or violence; attendance only by supportive people | Session notes; confidential reporting route | Month 6 and 12 |
| Supply is reliable | No stock-outs recorded | Repeated stock-outs | Mobile clinic and clinic stock records | Monthly |

**Pause rule:** if any safety incident links to the family sessions, stop them and review before continuing. Set success thresholds with the donor at month 3, once the power mapping is complete. We have not invented targets here.

## Data gaps

- ⚠️ Data gap: who holds power over young women's decisions, and how this differs by age, marital status, work and schooling. This could change which activities the project needs. Recommended action: the month 1 to 3 power mapping.
- ⚠️ Data gap: baseline figures on current contraceptive use and clinic experiences in these settlements. Recommended action: collect before the first activities begin.
- ⚠️ Data gap: no evidence yet on supply continuity at the two clinics. Recommended action: ask the clinics for stock records.
