I need a theory of change for a funding proposal, and I have until tomorrow morning.

A Member Association (MA) plans a three-year project (illustrative, for this exercise) in two informal urban settlements of one city. About 9,000 young women aged 15-24 live there. Many have left school early, many work in informal jobs, and many depend on a partner or a relative for money and permission to travel. The nearest public clinic is a 40-minute walk or a costly bus ride, and clinic staff are often unkind to unmarried young women asking for contraception.

Long-term outcome the MA wants to contribute to: young women in these settlements decide for themselves whether and when to use contraception, and can get it without shame or third-party permission.

What the project can deliver: 24 trained peer educators, a weekly mobile clinic day in each settlement, a training course on respectful care for staff at the two nearest public clinics, and monthly sessions with partners and mothers-in-law. It cannot change national law or fund transport.

Timeframe: 3 years, with markers at 12 months and at the end of year 3.

The MA has a short needs assessment (interviews with 40 young women) but no earlier theory of change. We have no analysis yet of who holds power over these young women's decisions.

Write a working theory of change of about 800 to 1,200 words for programme staff and the donor, who are not MEL specialists. Give the main pathway, the assumptions we are making, and a plan to test the riskiest ones. Keep it practical.
