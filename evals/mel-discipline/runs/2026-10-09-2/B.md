# Extended youth clinic hours: extend to all eight districts?

**Recommendation: do not extend to all eight districts yet. Extend to two or three more districts in 2026, keep a comparison group, and decide on full roll-out once the numbers separate the effect of the hours from the effect of the national campaign.** The pilot data show a rise in young clients, but they cannot show that the longer hours caused it.

Young clients (aged 15 to 24) rose 19.8% in the two pilot districts and 16.7% in the district without the pilot. A national campaign ran in all three districts in mid-2025.

## What the numbers show

| District | Pilot? | 2024 | 2025 | Change | Change % |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | yes | 1,240 | 1,610 | +370 | +29.8% |
| B | yes | 860 | 905 | +45 | +5.2% |
| C | no | 1,020 | 1,190 | +170 | +16.7% |
| A + B together | yes | 2,100 | 2,515 | +415 | +19.8% |

1. **Start with district C.** It had no extended hours, and its young clients still grew by 16.7%. That growth is our best estimate of what would have happened anyway, including the effect of the campaign.
2. **Apply that growth to the pilot districts.** At C's rate, A would have reached about 1,447 clients and B about 1,003. A beat that by about 163 clients. B fell short by about 98.
3. **Add the two together.** The pilot districts finished about 65 clients ahead of the no-pilot pattern, a 3.1-point gap. That is small against 2,100 clients, and one district produced all of it.

## The strongest objection, and our answer

*Objection: district A grew by almost 30%, nearly double district C. Surely the hours worked.*

That may be true, and we do not rule it out. But three things stop us from calling it proof:
- **B did not follow A.** If longer hours drive attendance, both pilot districts should show it. Clinic size, location, staffing, or how well the new hours were publicised could each explain the difference.
- **We have one year of data.** We do not know whether A was already growing faster than C before 2025.
- **Three districts are a thin base.** One unusual district can move the result.

District A is a good lead. It is not yet a reason to spend on five more districts.

## What the wider evidence says

A systematic review of youth-friendly family planning services found 19 studies. Six measured outcomes, and most of those showed gains in contraceptive use and client satisfaction. The authors still concluded that the evidence is limited and that more rigorous studies are needed. The review treated clinic hours as one of several ways to improve access, so it gives no separate estimate for longer hours. It also covered studies from the United States, Canada, Europe, Australia and New Zealand only, and its search ended in February 2011.

The World Health Organization (WHO) updated its global standards for quality health-care services for adolescents in October 2025. They set nine standards for the level of quality adolescent services should reach. The standards support the direction of this pilot. The WHO page does not mention opening hours, so it cannot settle the question.

Our reading: longer hours fit the evidence on youth-friendly services, but no source we opened shows how many extra clients they bring.

## What to do next

1. **Choose two or three more districts** for extended hours in 2026. Pick them partly for need, and keep at least two similar districts on current hours as a comparison.
2. **Count clients by time slot** (weekday daytime, evening, Saturday). Today the data cannot show whether the new hours reached young people who could not come before.
3. **Count new and returning clients separately.** A rise in repeat visits means something different from a rise in new clients.
4. **Find out why B lagged.** Ask B's staff and a few young clients. This costs little and may explain the gap.
5. **Record the extra staff cost per additional young client.** We do not have this figure, and managers need it for the full roll-out decision.
6. **Review in early 2027.** If the gap over comparison districts holds in at least two of the new districts, extend to all eight.

**What could change this advice:** a clear local reason for B's low result, earlier-year data showing A was not already growing faster (ask the MA for it this week), or very low extra cost.
