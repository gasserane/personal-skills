# Extended youth clinic hours: extend to all eight districts?

**Do not extend to all eight districts yet. Extend to two or three more districts next year, keep the rest as a comparison, and check two numbers we already hold before you commit.** The pilot data show a real rise in young clients, but they do not show that the longer hours caused it, and the two pilot districts gave very different results.

## What the numbers show

| District | Pilot? | Young clients (15-24), 2024 | 2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | yes | 1,240 | 1,610 | +370 (+29.8%) |
| B | yes | 860 | 905 | +45 (+5.2%) |
| C | no | 1,020 | 1,190 | +170 (+16.7%) |

A national social-media campaign on contraception ran in all three districts in mid-2025. District C had no new hours, yet its young clients grew by one sixth. So the campaign, or something else common to all districts, explains a large share of the growth.

The fair comparison is pilot growth minus the growth we would expect anyway. Taking C as the guide, A did about 13 percentage points better. B did about 11 points worse. Together the two pilot districts grew 19.8% (2,100 to 2,515 clients), against 16.7% in C. That is roughly 65 extra young clients across both districts, from a gap of 3.1 points.

## Why we cannot say the hours worked

- **One comparison district.** C may differ from A and B in size, location or clinic staffing. Growth of 16.7% in one district is a thin yardstick.
- **A and B point in opposite directions.** If longer hours worked, we would expect gains in both. We do not know why B stayed flat. Possible reasons are poor promotion of the new hours, transport after dark, or a smaller pool of young people nearby.
- **The campaign may not hit every district equally.** Young people in A may have responded more strongly for reasons unrelated to opening times.
- **Clients are not visits.** The data count clients, not whether they came in the evening or on a Saturday.

The strongest objection to waiting is that A's result is large and the evidence base for convenient hours is sound, so delay costs young people access. We accept that point for A-type districts. That is why we recommend extending in more districts now, rather than pausing.

## What the wider evidence says

The World Health Organization (WHO) and UNAIDS global standards for adolescent health care treat convenient service access as part of facility quality. They are benchmarks of good practice, not proof that longer hours raise attendance. A WHO-linked review of services in low- and middle-income countries found the strongest results for packages. These combined trained providers, adolescent-friendly facilities and information spread through communities, schools and media. Training alone had limited evidence. The review does not isolate opening hours as a single lever.

This matches what we see. Longer hours are most likely to work as one part of a package, and the campaign may have supplied the demand side. ⚠️ Data gap: we found no study that tests extended hours alone against a comparison group. This matters because the MA is paying for staff time on a feature that no study isolates. Recommended action: treat our own comparison as the best evidence available and design it well.

## What to do

1. **Pull two figures from existing records within four weeks.** First, the share of young clients in A and B who came in evening or Saturday slots. Second, 2024 and 2025 young-client counts for the five districts without pilots. If A's extra clients came mostly in the new slots, and the five districts grew by about 17%, the hours effect is credible.
2. **Choose two or three new districts next year.** Pick at least one that resembles B, so you learn what went wrong there. Leave the remaining districts on current hours as comparison.
3. **Ask B's clinic staff and young clients why uptake stayed flat.** Run two short group discussions. Ask about promotion, safety and transport after dark.
4. **Record cost per additional young client.** We have no figures on staff, security or opening costs. ⚠️ Data gap: without them, management cannot weigh the benefit against the price.
5. **Set the decision rule now.** Extend to all eight districts if the new districts grow at least five points faster than the comparison districts, and if clients use the extended slots. Otherwise, adapt before scaling.

**Worked example.** If a new district grows 24% while the comparison districts grow 17%, the gap is seven points. That passes the rule. If it grows 19%, the gap is two points. Hold and investigate.

## What would change this advice

The recommendation shifts to "extend to all eight now" if the five other districts grew near 17% and A and B's extra clients came mainly in evening and Saturday slots. It shifts to "stop" if the five other districts grew as fast as A, which would point to the campaign.
