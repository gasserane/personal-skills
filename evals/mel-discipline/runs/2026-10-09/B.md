# Should we extend evening and Saturday hours to all eight districts?

**Recommendation: do not extend to all eight districts yet. Extend to three more districts next year, keep at least two districts on normal hours for comparison, and fix whatever held back district B first.** The pilot data cannot show that the longer hours caused the rise in young clients, because a national campaign ran at the same time and the two pilot districts gave opposite results.

## What the numbers show

We compared young clients (aged 15 to 24) in 2024 and 2025. We recomputed every figure from the monitoring table.

| District | Pilot? | 2024 | 2025 | Change | Change in % |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | yes | 1,240 | 1,610 | +370 | +29.8% |
| B | yes | 860 | 905 | +45 | +5.2% |
| C | no | 1,020 | 1,190 | +170 | +16.7% |
| Pilot districts together | yes | 2,100 | 2,515 | +415 | +19.8% |

1. **Every district grew, including district C, which kept normal hours.** Its 16.7% growth is our best guide to what the campaign and other trends did on their own.
2. **The pilot districts together beat district C by only 3.1 percentage points** (19.8% against 16.7%). One comparison district is too few to trust that gap.
3. **The two pilot districts disagree.** District A beat district C by 13.1 points. District B fell 11.5 points below it. A real effect of the hours should show in both.

## What the evidence on youth-friendly services says

Hours that suit young people are an accepted part of good practice. The WHO and UNAIDS global standards ask facilities to offer "convenient operating hours and flexible appointment procedures" (Nair et al. 2015, Standard 5). So the idea is sound.

The same literature warns that one change rarely moves use on its own. A review found that services raise adolescent use only when four things come together: friendly providers, welcoming facilities, outreach, and community support. Most programmes do not deliver all four (Chandra-Mouli et al. 2015). That is a narrative review, not a systematic one, and it does not discuss opening hours. Treat it as a caution, not proof.

This fits district B. If B had weaker outreach or fewer trained providers, longer hours alone would not bring young people in. We do not know this, because the data do not say.

## The strongest objection, and our answer

*Objection:* district A grew almost 30%. Waiting a year denies young people access they clearly want.

*Answer:* district A also had the campaign, and it beat district C by 13 points, which is encouraging. But a rollout to eight districts costs staff time and evening security in every one. If the effect belongs to district A's own circumstances, that cost buys little elsewhere. A staged rollout loses little, because longer hours are easy to start and easy to stop.

## What to do next

1. **Ask the MA this month for 2025 visits by time slot** in districts A and B (weekday daytime, evening, Saturday). If A's growth came mainly from evening and Saturday visits, that is the strongest evidence we can get cheaply. If it came from daytime visits, the hours did not drive it.
2. **Find out why district B lagged.** Ask staff and young clients about awareness of the new hours, provider attitudes, and transport after dark. Ask young people first.
3. **Extend to three more districts**, chosen to differ from each other (for example, one rural, one urban, one with low youth use). Keep the rest on normal hours as a comparison group.
4. **Track the same figures monthly** in all districts, with the same age groups.
5. **Decide on full rollout once those results are in.** Extend if the new districts beat the comparison districts by a clear margin in most of them, not in one.

## What we could not verify

⚠️ Data gap: the table is illustrative and covers three of eight districts, with one non-pilot district — one comparison district cannot separate the hours from district-specific factors — collect the same data for all eight districts for 2024 and 2025.

⚠️ Data gap: no data on which hours the new clients came — it is the direct test of whether the hours worked — request the time-slot split in step 1.

⚠️ Data gap: WHO ran a consultation on a second edition of the standards in 2024, and we could not confirm that a final second edition exists — the first edition is cited as the latest we could confirm — check WHO's publications page before quoting the standards externally.
